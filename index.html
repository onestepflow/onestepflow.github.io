When One Step Is Enough:
An Empirical Study of Euler Integration
in Flow-Matching Policies

Araya Inc., Tokyo, Japan

* Equal contribution

Accepted at the 10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026)

November 9–12, 2026 · Austin, Texas, USA

Paper BibTeX

Project Overview

For large, sufficiently trained vision-language-action models, a single Euler step largely matches multi-step task success, with 1.6× to 4.8× faster action generation across the evaluated VLAs. No retraining, distillation, or auxiliary objectives are required.

Abstract

Flow matching is now the dominant approach for vision-language action (VLA) robot policies, thanks to its expressiveness and simple training objective. However, inference for standard flow matching requires iteratively solving an ODE, limiting the control frequency at deployment. The prevailing VLA training recipe relies on rectified flow matching whose target are straight lines between source and target samples. In general the resulting paths are not straight requiring, in principle, multiple integration steps for a more accurate velocity integration. We challenge this hypothesis empirically, arguing that for robot action generation this needs not be the case. We show that across six flow-matching VLAs, one-step action generation largely matches multi-step sampling at scale: the multi-step refinement that these models are typically evaluated with confers little to no consistent advantage at inference. Our experiments show that this holds as long as models are sufficiently trained and have enough capacity. Practically, the implication is that for large VLAs, the single-step policy comes essentially for free from the multi-step model, enabling low-latency deployment without retraining, distillation, or auxiliary objectives.

Experimental Results

We compare six publicly released VLAs, spanning 450M to 4.7B parameters, and a 211M DiT baseline across four simulation benchmarks. For each model and benchmark, we use the same checkpoint and matched seeds, changing only the number of Euler integration steps.

Key Findings

  • Step-invariance holds at scale. π₀.₅ and XR-0 are equivalent within 3 pp on every simulation benchmark with an available checkpoint. Smaller models show more mixed results, especially on longer tasks.
  • Training maturity and VLM features matter. Robot-action pretraining is not required. With useful visual-language features, a randomly initialized action expert can still reach the step-invariant regime.
  • Task success and behavioral diversity can differ. On D3IL Avoiding, one-step inference can preserve success while narrowing the range of successful strategies.

Real-World Evaluation

We evaluate four kitchen-style tasks with a Franka Emika Panda arm using paired, blinded A/B testing. The suite includes 4,800 rollouts, with 100 trials per task for each model and inference variant.

Multi-step inference confers no systematic advantage: pooled gaps are small and run in both directions. Only 3 of 24 task–model comparisons show a significant McNemar difference. The physical evaluation budget does not establish equivalence within ±3 pp, and task- and model-specific exceptions remain.

Inference Latency

A single Euler step reduces per-chunk inference latency by 1.6× to 4.8× on an NVIDIA A100. Speedups depend on the relative cost of the VLM backbone and action head.
